# SPOT — translation glossary (az → ru / en)

Every translator uses these. A term that appears here is translated exactly this
way everywhere; consistency matters more than any one translator's preference.

## Voice
- The app speaks informally, second person singular: Azerbaijani «sən».
  Russian: **«ты»** (never «вы»). English: plain second person, friendly, short.
- Legal documents (terms, privacy, community rules) are the exception: formal, precise.
- Keep sentences as short as the Azerbaijani. A button stays a button-length label.

## Honesty rules — these are product rules, not style
SPOT never states something it did not really obtain. The Azerbaijani draws hard
distinctions and the translation must keep every one of them:
- A failed read is NOT «nothing here». «Yüklənmədi — bu «yoxdur» demək deyil» must
  stay «Не загрузилось — это не значит, что их нет» / «Didn't load — that doesn't mean
  there are none». Never shorten it to «Nothing here».
- «yalnız bu cihazda» (only on this device) vs «serverdə» (on the server) — keep exact.
- A failure message is never softened into a success or a neutral note.
- «pulsuz» = free. SPOT takes NO payments: never introduce words like «buy», «pay»,
  «purchase», «subscription», «оплатить», «купить».

## Mechanics
- Keep every `{placeholder}` EXACTLY as written — same name, same braces. Move it to
  wherever the target grammar needs it, but never rename, drop or add one.
- Keep «», —, emoji and punctuation style. Russian uses «ёлочки»; English uses "quotes".
- Keep `[DOLDURULMALI: …]` markers verbatim — they are blanks the owner must fill.
- Keep brand and product names untranslated: SPOT, Google, Apple, WhatsApp, VÖEN.
- Counted strings (marked `counted`) get plural forms:
  Russian `ru_one` (1, 21, 31…), `ru_few` (2-4, 22-24…), `ru_many` (0, 5-20, 25…);
  English `en_one`, `en_other`.
